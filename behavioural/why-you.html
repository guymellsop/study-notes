Study/Interview answers

Why you? Why leave your job?

Mock Q11 · part 2 Pass, part 1 Partial · the two questions that decide applications · 5 min

In one breath

Refuse the axis. They invite a comparison on "years at a studio", where you lose by definition. Two sentences on the gap, then straight to what you have: breadth across art, tech and design, and decisions you can show. Never argue the other candidate's case.

"Why are you leaving?" (strong, keep it)

"I'm not looking to leave my job, I'm looking to pursue my dream job." No bitterness, clear positive motivation.

  • Add the reassurance: "I know what I'd be giving up, and I've decided it's worth it, because it's the work I want to be doing." Otherwise "good pay, great boss" plants "they'll regret it in six months".
  • Have a salary number ready.
  • Swap "just a job" for "it pays well and I do it properly, but it isn't the career I want to build."

"Why you over someone with a degree and 3 years?"

Cut this sentence

"If another applicant is more appealing and their asking price isn't too steep, going with them is exactly what I would do." You mean it as humility; it reads as not rating your own case. With 200 applicants, conviction is often the tiebreaker. And don't open with why you're allowed to be in the room. Openings are what get remembered.

40-second answer

"Someone with three years at a studio knows how a team ships. I don't, and I won't pretend otherwise, but that's also the fastest thing to pick up on the job. What I'd bring that a three-year junior often doesn't is breadth and evidence. I've done the art, the tech and the design, which matters at a small studio where nobody gets to do only one thing. And I can show my decisions, not just my results: my design docs record what I considered, what I rejected and why."

The bigger pattern

You have real conviction talking about the craft (Q10) and lose it the moment the question is about you ("vibe-coded", "I overstated my C++", "hire the other person"). Practising that is worth more than any technical item.

Rule for the room: state the gap once, plainly, with no adjectives, and never volunteer a second one.
